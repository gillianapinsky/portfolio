02  CONVERGENT DENTAL · INTERNSHIP

Sheet Metal Mounting Plates

Two sheet-metal mounting plates, for the air module and the galvos, designed for a new internal chassis in the Helios dental laser and fit-tested as physical prototypes.

Role
Mechanical Engineering Intern
Context
Convergent Dental, Helios laser
Tools
SOLIDWORKSSheet metal designAssembly designDFM

Problem

Convergent Dental was moving the Helios laser to a new internal chassis structure, with the goal of decreasing manufacturing time and reducing repair complexity for field engineers.

I designed the sheet-metal plates that mount two modules inside it, 1. the air module and 2. the galvos, and took each from sketch through CAD, release drawings and a fit-tested physical prototype. I did the concept sketches, SOLIDWORKS models, chassis fit checks and release drawings.

+ [ DESIGN PROCESS ]

01Concept Generation

I sketched mounting layouts for both modules before modeling.

02Iterative CAD Design

I modeled both plates as sheet-metal parts in SOLIDWORKS. The air-module plate is a large two-level plate with an offset bend; the galvo plate is a long L-shaped bracket with standoffs.

03Fit to Chassis

I mounted each module on its plate in the SOLIDWORKS chassis assembly to check that it fit inside the new structure before releasing drawings.

04Release Drawings

I released a drawing package for each plate: an assembly drawing with BOM, a dimensioned part drawing and a flat pattern. Specified material: aluminum 5052-H32, 0.1 in (2.54 mm) thick, with a clear chromate finish per MIL-C-5541 Type II, Class 1A (RoHS).

05Final Sheet Metal Prototype

Both plates were made as physical sheet-metal prototypes, and each was physically fit-tested in the chassis.

Results & Impact

  • Designed two sheet-metal mounting plates, for the air module and the galvos, within the redesigned Helios chassis.
  • Released assembly drawings with BOMs, dimensioned part drawings and flat patterns for fabrication.
  • Produced physical sheet-metal prototypes of both plates and fit-tested them in the chassis.
  • Intended benefit: simpler assembly and maintenance in the new chassis. Changes in assembly or service time were not measured.
